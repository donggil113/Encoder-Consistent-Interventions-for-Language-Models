\begin{abstract}
  Steering a language model with a sparse autoencoder (SAE) usually adds a decoder
  direction to an activation and describes the edit by the feature it names. We audit that
  description twice: inside the SAE, where re-encoding the edited activation shows the
  achieved target change and the drift on other features, and outside it, in behavior
  that the SAE cannot certify. We decompose edit fidelity into a scale error, interference
  on features that were already active, and drift from features that switch on or off, and
  we collect the standard linear-algebra conditions under which a least-norm correction on
  a target and a protected feature set exists; neither the solver nor its null-space
  constraint is new. In controlled toy models, one axis of which is exploratory, the correction removes interference only when
  the protected set is small relative to the dimension and the edit is small; otherwise it
  moves drift onto newly activated features, and bounded active-set repair satisfies its
  constraints only by abandoning the target, while a constructed counterexample shows that
  exact agreement with the SAE encoder can coexist with worse control of an external
  readout. Measurements on a public model--SAE pair and a held-out behavioral task are
  specified and implemented but have not been run.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Sparse autoencoders (SAEs) are used to steer language models by editing an internal
activation along a learned feature. Common implementations add a multiple of the feature's
decoder vector to the residual stream, or clamp the feature inside the SAE reconstruction
while keeping the reconstruction error
\citep{templeton2024scaling,obrien2025steering,wu2025axbench}. Either way the edit is
reported in the SAE's vocabulary: ``feature $j$ was increased by $\alpha$''. This paper
asks what that sentence certifies.

It can fail in two places. Inside the SAE, the encoder that defines ``feature $j$'' need
not read the decoder vector back as a pure change of $j$: encoder and decoder directions
differ \citep{chanin2025absorption,oneill2025compute}, and the decoder of one feature can
excite others. Outside the SAE, behavior need not follow even an internally exact edit.
External failures are documented. SAE decoder steering scores below a difference-in-means
vector on AxBench \citep{wu2025axbench}, features that detect a concept are often not the
features whose decoder produces it \citep{arad2025saes}.
Encoder--decoder misalignment has already been named as one contributor to SAE steering
failures, next to off-manifold artifacts \citep{luo2026learning}.

Two nearby lines of work shape what can be new here. SAE-targeted steering fits a global
linear map from steering vectors to their measured effects on SAE features and steers
along a column of that map; the authors report that a pseudo-inverse of the map
performed worse \citep{chalnev2024improving}. And the projector
$I - J^{\top}(JJ^{\top})^{\dagger}J$ onto directions that leave a set of SAE features
unchanged has been used to show that behavior can be restored while those feature
readouts stay fixed \citep{cui2026sae}. Least-norm edits that set linear readouts to target
values are standard in subspace interventions \citep{geiger2024finding,wu2024reft}, and
null-space protection of preserved outputs is standard in model editing
\citep{fang2025alphaedit,sheng2026alphasteer}.

We therefore do not propose a new steering method. We ask three narrower questions.
(i) How much of the gap between an edit's intended and achieved SAE change is a scale error
that rescaling removes, how much is interference on features that were already active, and
how much comes from features switching on or off? (ii) When does a local correction on a
target and a protected set exist, and what does it cost? (iii) Does agreement with the
SAE's own encoder say anything about external effects?

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item An audit definition (\cref{sec:setup}): intended versus achieved change after
  re-encoding at the same layer, split into target error, drift on originally active
  non-target features (kept or deactivated), and drift from newly activated features. It
  separates equal-norm from achieved-target-matched comparisons and keeps infeasible cases
  as rows.
  \item A compact account of feasibility and its limits (\cref{sec:analysis}), assembled from
  textbook results on least-norm solutions, the Fredholm alternative and trust-region
  subproblems, plus elementary facts about ReLU, JumpReLU and TopK boundaries. None of these
  statements is new.
  \item Controlled toy evidence, including negative results (\cref{sec:toy}). In a
  pre-registered dense ReLU setting, the correction had higher total drift than a rescaled
  decoder in most instances of most cells, because it moved drift onto newly activated
  features. An
  exploratory sparser setting, added after inspecting development seeds, showed the reverse
  at small doses.
  \item A constructed counterexample (\cref{sec:proxy}): the correction makes the internal
  metric exact, yet the external readout gets worse in every instance where the decoder,
  rather than the encoder, matches it.
  \item A real-model protocol (\cref{sec:real}): a source-verified contract for GPT-2 small
  and a public residual SAE, document-level splits, calibration-only doses, adapters, and a
  benign behavioral task whose label does not use the SAE. These measurements are \notrun.
\end{itemize}
We make no claim that the correction improves behavior.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{SAE steering and its evaluation.}
Feature steering by clamping or adding decoder directions was demonstrated at scale by
\citet{templeton2024scaling} and applied to refusal by \citet{obrien2025steering}.
AxBench implements SAE steering as $h + \alpha\, w_{\mathrm{dec}}$ with a unit-norm decoder
direction, scores concept, instruction relevance and fluency with a judge, and reports a
mean steering score of 0.165 for SAEs against 0.239 for difference-in-means
\citep{wu2025axbench}. \citet{arad2025saes} separate input features (whose activations track
a concept) from output features (whose decoder produces it) and find that the two rarely
coincide. \citet{bayat2025steering} edit in the SAE latent space, re-apply the activation
there and decode with the reconstruction error added back. \citet{makelov2024towards} fit
decoder-space edits to counterfactual activations by least squares and score them by
next-token agreement. \citet{bhalla2025towards} evaluate encoder--decoder interventions by
their effect on model outputs. These works measure behavior. They do not re-encode the
edited activation, so they cannot say whether the SAE itself registers the intended change.

\paragraph{Targeting SAE features.}
SAE-TS measures a steering vector's effect as
$y = \mathbb{E}_{\text{steered}}[f(x)] - \mathbb{E}_{\text{unsteered}}[f(x)]$ over SAE
activations of generated rollouts. It learns $\hat y = xM + b$ from 50k decoder vectors and
steers with $s = M_j/\|M_j\| - \lambda\, Mb/\|Mb\|$ \citep{chalnev2024improving}. Its
appendix reports that the pseudo-inverse of the learned $M$ is worse on all nine tasks.
From the text we could not determine whether the steering vector is present when rollouts
are re-encoded. Feature-guided activation additions reuse this effect map for multi-feature
targets \citep{soo2025interpretable}. Our audit differs in three ways: it re-encodes each
edited activation at the edit layer, it uses the exact per-input encoder rows instead of a
fitted global map, and it controls for edit norm. The pseudo-inverse result is prior
evidence against our correction, not for it.

\paragraph{Encoders are not inverses of decoders.}
Feature absorption leaves holes in encoder recall that the decoder does not show
\citep{chanin2025absorption}. A linear--nonlinear encoder has a provable amortisation gap
\citep{oneill2025compute}. Encoding a bare steering vector is dominated by the encoder bias
\citep{mayne2024can}. \citet{luo2026learning} state that feature descriptions come from the
encoder while steering directions come from the decoder, and conclude that off-manifold
artifacts, ``not just encoder-decoder misalignment'', contribute to SAE steering failures.
We treat misalignment as known. What we add is a same-layer decomposition of its
consequences and a test (not yet run) of how much it explains.

\paragraph{Predicting side effects.}
\citet{duan2026preintervention} steer with $h' = h + \alpha d_f$ at the final token and
predict collateral changes on a downstream-layer SAE from decoder geometry, activation
statistics and logit effects. From the text we could not find a measurement of the
target's own same-layer activation. \citet{khan2026look} predict the smallest coefficient
that shifts a keyword-based refusal rate from decoder neighbor density and related
statistics. Whether same-layer re-encoded drift predicts failure beyond such geometry
predictors is one of our open questions.

\paragraph{Least-norm and null-space interventions.}
With an orthonormal $R$, the distributed interchange intervention
$b + R^{\top}(Rs - Rb)$ \citep{geiger2024finding} and LoReFT's
$h + R^{\top}(Wh + b - Rh)$ \citep{wu2024reft} are least-norm edits that set readouts $Rh$ to
target values. Our correction has the same form, with non-orthogonal SAE encoder rows in
place of $R$ and a fixed target. AlphaEdit projects weight updates onto the null space of
preserved keys \citep{fang2025alphaedit}, and AlphaSteer constrains activation steering to a
null space of benign activations \citep{sheng2026alphasteer}. LEACE and MiMiC are least-change
edits under readout or distributional constraints
\citep{belrose2025leace,singh2025representation}. \citet{cui2026sae} hold defended SAE
features fixed with $I - A(A^{\top}A)^{\dagger}A^{\top}$ within a layer and
$I - J_t^{\top}(J_tJ_t^{\top})^{\dagger}J_t$ across layers while recovering suppressed
behavior. This is our protected-set constraint used adversarially.
\citet{park2024linear} show that readout and intervention directions are related through a
metric (a causal inner product), so our Euclidean least-norm choice is one choice among
several.

\paragraph{Benchmarks.}
SAEBench's eight metrics \citep{karvonen2025saebench} and RAVEL's cause and isolation scores
\citep{huang2024ravel,chaudhary2024evaluating} evaluate interventions by their downstream
effects. On our reading, none of them measures intended versus achieved change after
re-encoding an edited activation. Jacobian SAEs compute sparse latent-to-latent Jacobians
through an MLP \citep{farnik2025jacobian}. With an identity in place of the MLP, that object
reduces to the $E_{\mathrm{active}}D_{\mathrm{active}}$ matrix that governs our linear
analysis.

\section{Problem Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{SAE.}
Let $x\in\R^d$ be an activation, written in the coordinates the SAE was trained on. The
encoder has pre-activations $p(x) = Ex + b$ with $E\in\R^{m\times d}$ and features
$a = \sigma(p)$, where $\sigma$ is ReLU, JumpReLU with threshold $\theta$, or TopK. The decoder
$D\in\R^{d\times m}$ has columns $D_j$. For the public SAE in \cref{sec:real},
$p(x) = W_{\mathrm{enc}}^{\top}(x - b_{\mathrm{dec}}) + b_{\mathrm{enc}}$, so
$E = W_{\mathrm{enc}}^{\top}$ and $b = b_{\mathrm{enc}} - W_{\mathrm{enc}}^{\top}b_{\mathrm{dec}}$.

\paragraph{Edit problem.}
An edit is specified by a target $j$, an intended post-activation change $\alpha>0$, and a
protected set $P$, which we take to be the non-target features active at $x$. The target
value $a_j+\alpha$ requires the pre-activation change
$\Delta p_j^{\mathrm{req}} = a_j + \alpha - p_j$ whenever the target ends up active. The
achieved change of an edit $\delta$ is $\Delta a = \sigma(p(x+\delta)) - \sigma(p(x))$.

\begin{definition}[Edit-fidelity decomposition]
\label{def:fidelity}
Let $A_0$ and $A_1$ be the active sets before and after the edit. The \emph{target error} is
$|\Delta a_j - \alpha|/\alpha$. The drift terms are $\ell_2$ norms of $\Delta a$ over three
disjoint groups of non-target features $i\neq j$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $D_{\mathrm{kept}}$ over $i\in A_0\cap A_1$;
  \item $D_{\mathrm{deact}}$ over $i\in A_0\setminus A_1$;
  \item $D_{\mathrm{new}}$ over $i\in A_1\setminus A_0$.
\end{itemize}
Originally active drift is $D_{\mathrm{orig}} = (D_{\mathrm{kept}}^2 + D_{\mathrm{deact}}^2)^{1/2}$,
and $D_{\mathrm{all}}$ is the norm over all $i\neq j$. All terms are reported relative to a
scale: $\alpha$ for target-matched comparisons and the norm budget $\rho$ for equal-norm
comparisons.
\end{definition}
For ReLU, JumpReLU and TopK, a feature inactive before and after has zero change. Hence
$D_{\mathrm{all}}^2 = D_{\mathrm{orig}}^2 + D_{\mathrm{new}}^2$. With $P = A_0\setminus\{j\}$,
$D_{\mathrm{orig}}$ and $D_{\mathrm{new}}$ are exactly the protected and unprotected leakage of
our toy study (checked numerically in our test suite).

\paragraph{Edits compared.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Decoder}: $\delta = cD_j$ with $c = \alpha$.
  \item \emph{Rescaled decoder}: $c$ chosen so that $\Delta p_j = \Delta p_j^{\mathrm{req}}$.
  In the toy study $c$ is computed per input. In the real protocol it is the weight-only
  $\alpha/(E_j D_j)$, which is deployable but not matched.
  \item \emph{Encoder gradient}: $\delta\propto E_j^{\top}$, the least-norm edit for the
  target row alone.
  \item \emph{\LN{} correction}: $\delta = M^{+}t$ with $M = E_{S\cup P}$ (the rows of $E$ for
  the target set $S=\{j\}$ and the protected set $P$) and
  $t = (\Delta p_j^{\mathrm{req}},0,\dots,0)$.
  \item \emph{\LN{} + repair}: up to three rounds that add newly crossed features to $P$ as
  equality constraints.
  \item \emph{Trust region}: $\arg\min_{\|\delta\|\le\tau}\|M\delta - t\|$.
  \item \emph{Random}: a random direction of the same norm, as a control.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Comparison modes and units.}
In an \emph{equal-norm} comparison, every direction is scaled to the same $\|\delta\|$. In a
\emph{target-matched} comparison, every direction is scaled so that $\Delta a_j = \alpha$.
Rows where no non-negative scale achieves this are kept as infeasible. The independent unit
is a random toy instance in \cref{sec:toy} and a held-out document in \cref{sec:real}.
Features, positions and base points within a unit are averaged before any summary.

\section{Analysis: Local Feasibility and Nonlinear Boundaries}
\label{sec:analysis}

All statements in this section are standard or elementary. We collect them because they
decide which toy outcomes are forced, and we prove them in \cref{app:proofs} only for
completeness.

\subsection{Linear encoders}
\begin{proposition}[Mismatch and rescaling]
\label{prop:mismatch}
If $\sigma$ is the identity, then $\Delta a = \alpha EDe_j$ for $\delta = \alpha D_j$.
Rescaling hits the target if and only if $(ED)_{jj}\neq 0$, and then leaves leakage
$\alpha\|(ED)_{Pj}\|/|(ED)_{jj}|$ on $P$. It therefore suffices if and only if column $j$ of
$ED$, restricted to $S\cup P$, is a multiple of $e_j$. For a tied encoder $E = D^{\top}$
whose decoder columns have pairwise cosine $c$, the leakage is $c\sqrt{m-1}\,\alpha$ both
before and after rescaling.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Least-norm solutions and infeasibility]
\label{prop:feasible}
$M\delta = t$ is solvable if and only if $t\perp\ker M^{\top}$. If $M$ has full row rank,
$\delta^\star = M^{\top}(MM^{\top})^{-1}t$ is the unique minimum-norm solution, and every
other solution has squared norm $\|\delta^\star\|^2 + \|n\|^2$ for some $n\in\ker M$.
Otherwise the residual $r = t - MM^{+}t$ certifies infeasibility: $M^{\top}r = 0$ and
$r^{\top}t = \|r\|^2 > 0$. In particular, a generic target is infeasible once
$|S\cup P| > \operatorname{rank}M$, which holds whenever $|S\cup P| > d$. Two identical
encoder rows with targets $(\alpha,0)$ leave residual $\alpha/\sqrt2$ in any dimension.
\end{proposition}
These are textbook facts \citep{golub2013matrix,bjorck1996numerical}.

\begin{corollary}[Three regimes]
\label{cor:regimes}
Let $P$ contain all non-target features.
\begin{itemize}
  \item If $E = D^{+}$ and $D$ has full column rank, then $\delta^\star = \alpha D_j$: the
  decoder edit is already least-norm.
  \item If $D$ has orthonormal columns and $E = \operatorname{diag}(s)D^{\top}$, then
  $\delta^\star$ is the rescaled decoder edit.
  \item If $E = D^{\top}$ with Gram matrix $G = D^{\top}D$, then
  $\|\delta^\star\|/\alpha = \sqrt{(G^{-1})_{jj}}\ge 1$, with equality if and only if
  $Ge_j\propto e_j$.
\end{itemize}
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Compute]
For same-layer edits, the Jacobian of $p$ restricted to $S\cup P$ is the gathered matrix
$E_{S\cup P}$. With $|S\cup P|\ll d$ it is small, so materializing it costs little.
Matrix-free solvers that use only Jacobian--vector and vector--Jacobian products, such as
CGLS/LSQR \citep{paige1982lsqr,bjorck1996numerical}, matter when the protected readouts sit at
a later layer. In the toy study, CGLS matched the dense solution to within \VfiveMaxDiff{}
(relative).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Nonlinear boundaries}
\begin{proposition}[ReLU piece exactness]
\label{prop:relu}
For ReLU, suppose no pre-activation, including the target's, changes sign between $x$ and
$x+\delta$. Then $\Delta a = \operatorname{diag}(\mathbb{1}[p>0])E\delta$ exactly. If the
pre-activations of $P = A_0\setminus\{j\}$ are held fixed, then
$D_{\mathrm{kept}} = D_{\mathrm{deact}} = 0$, and all remaining drift is $D_{\mathrm{new}}$. It
comes from inactive features with $(E\delta)_i > -p_i$. The almost-everywhere Jacobian row
of an inactive target is zero, so a solve on post-activation Jacobians cannot activate it.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[JumpReLU and TopK]
\label{prop:jump}
A JumpReLU feature cannot take values in $(0,\theta]$, and crossing $\theta$ changes the
activation by at least $\theta$ where the almost-everywhere derivative predicts no change.
Under TopK with exactly $k$ positive active features, an inactive target can enter only by
evicting at least one of them. In both cases the local derivative is not a global inverse.
For TopK, comparing active sets at the two endpoints only lower-bounds the number of
changes along the path.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Norm budget]
\label{prop:tr}
$\min_{\|\delta\|\le\tau}\|M\delta - t\|$ is convex. Its solution is $M^{+}t$ if
$\|M^{+}t\|\le\tau$. Otherwise it is $(M^{\top}M+\lambda I)^{-1}M^{\top}t$ with the unique
$\lambda>0$ such that the solution has norm $\tau$ \citep{more1983computing}.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{What agreement with the encoder can certify}
\label{sec:certify}
Where it is feasible, the \LN{} correction makes $D_{\mathrm{orig}} = 0$ and the target error
zero by construction, as measured with the same encoder that defines the constraint. That
guarantee says nothing about features outside $S\cup P$
(\cref{prop:relu}, and \cref{prop:feasible} with subset protection). It also says nothing
about any readout other than $E$. If an external readout $R$ differs from $E$ on
$S\cup P$, an edit exact for $E$ is generally not exact for $R$.
\Cref{sec:proxy} turns this remark into a concrete counterexample. It is also why we do not
count gains in internal metrics as evidence of control.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Controlled study design}
\label{sec:toy}
We implemented the toy study in the Python standard library, because no numerical
libraries were available in the approved environment. The design, fixed in a configuration
file before the reported run, was as follows.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Pre-registered before the reported run:} verification hypotheses, metrics, the
  independent unit (one random draw of $(D, E, b)$), a budget of 120\,s on two threads, and
  stop conditions.
  \item \emph{Development seeds 0--2:} used only for debugging and not reported.
  \item \emph{Reported seeds 1000--1019:} run once, giving 20 instances per cell.
\end{itemize}
After the development seeds and before the reported run, we made one change: we added an
exploratory sparsity axis and made two feasibility checks explicit (\cref{app:toy}).

All \FeasTotalPass/\FeasTotal{} feasibility checks and the verification identities of
\cref{sec:analysis} passed (\cref{tab:feas}). Among them, the ReLU prediction is exact in
all \VsixRows{} rows without crossings, with maximum error \VsixMaxErr. These checks show
that the code is correct; they are not findings.

The reported suite took 76--77\,s on one thread. After a bug fix that touched only the
summary code, a rerun reproduced all raw files bit for bit. Every number below is
re-aggregated from those raw files.

\subsection{Linear regimes: rescaling versus interference}
\label{sec:linear}
\Cref{tab:linear} places seven linear regimes into the classes predicted by
\cref{prop:mismatch,prop:feasible}. A class is assigned only if it holds in all 20
instances.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{R0} has no mismatch. Steering along the encoder row nevertheless leaks
  \EncGradLeakRzero{} here, so ``encoder direction'' does not mean ``encoder-consistent''
  once other features count.
  \item \emph{R1} is a pure scale error (decoder target error \RoneDecTgt). Rescaling and the
  \LN{} correction coincide.
  \item \emph{R2} is pure interference. The leakage is \RtwoDecLeak{} $= 0.3\sqrt7$, and
  rescaling cannot change it. The \LN{} correction removes it at norm ratio \RtwoLnNorm{}
  $=\sqrt{(G^{-1})_{jj}}$. At the decoder's norm, the trust region reaches target error
  \RtwoTrTgt{} with leakage \RtwoTrLeak.
  \item \emph{R4a} protects all 47 non-targets with $d=16$ and is infeasible (median
  relative residual \RfouraRes; every row at least \RfouraResRowMin). The least-squares
  solution gives up the target (error \RfouraLnTgt).
  \item \emph{R4b and R5} protect 7 features. This zeroes their drift but \emph{raises}
  total leakage. In R4b it goes from \RfourbDecLeak{} to \RfourbLnLeak{} at norm ratio
  \RfourbLnNorm. In R5 it goes from \RfiveRescLeak{} (rescaled) to \RfiveLnLeak{} (\LN).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Active sets and norm budgets (pre-registered)}
\label{sec:activeset}
This setting has $d=16$ and $m=64$, an encoder halfway between tied and biorthogonal, and
base points built from three decoder atoms. The median number of active features is
\DenseReluActMed{} (range \DenseReluActMin--\DenseReluActMax) for ReLU and
\DenseJumpActMed{} for JumpReLU. This is dense: $|S\cup P|$ is a large fraction of $d$.
\Cref{tab:relu} gives the ReLU decomposition.

The \LN{} correction removes most originally active drift. Newly activated drift grows,
however, because the correction's larger norm pushes inactive features over threshold.
Its total drift was lower than the rescaled decoder's in \DenseReluActQWins,
\DenseReluActOneWins{} and \DenseReluActFourWins{} instances for active targets at
$\alpha = 0.25, 1, 4$, and in \DenseReluInactQWins, \DenseReluInactOneWins{} and
\DenseReluInactFourWins{} for inactive targets.

Bounded repair reached a crossing-free edit in \RepairConvRJMin--\RepairConvRJMax{} of
\RepairRowsPerCell{} rows per ReLU or JumpReLU cell, but it did so by abandoning the target:
median target error \RepairTgtMin--\RepairTgtMax{} over range-feasible cells. For TopK it
converged in only \RepairConvTKMin--\RepairConvTKMax{} of \RepairRowsPerCell{} rows per cell,
because eviction is rank-based (\cref{prop:jump}). As \cref{prop:relu} predicts, the naive
post-activation Jacobian never moved an inactive target. With JumpReLU, an inactive target
at $\alpha = 0.25<\theta = 0.3$ was unattainable for every method.

At the decoder's norm, the trust region had lower combined error than the decoder in
\TrWinsMin--\TrWinsMax{} of 20 instances per ReLU/JumpReLU cell (\cref{tab:budget}). This
comparison is circular: the trust region minimizes the same internal quantity it is scored
on.

\subsection{A counterexample: internal agreement without external control}
\label{sec:proxy}
A ground-truth dictionary $A$ ($d=16$, $m=8$, pairwise cosine $0.3$) defines an external
readout $z = A^{+}x$. The SAE is a noisy estimate of this dictionary. We consider three
cases: the encoder is exact ($E = A^{+}$), the decoder is exact ($D = A$), or both are noisy.
In every case the \LN{} correction drives the internal error to below $10^{-6}$ (\cref{tab:proxy}).
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Encoder exact:} the external error falls from \TxEncAccDecExt{} to
  \TxEncAccLnExt{} (better in \TxEncAccWins{} instances).
  \item \emph{Decoder exact:} it rises from \TxDecAccDecExt{} to \TxDecAccLnExt{} (better in
  \TxDecAccWins).
  \item \emph{Both noisy:} it is unchanged (better in \TxBothWins; median paired difference
  \TxBothDiff).
\end{itemize}
The outcome is fixed by construction. Its role is to show that the internal metric
measures agreement with the encoder, not with behavior. It is consistent with the finding
that behavior can move while SAE readouts are held fixed \citep{cui2026sae}.

\subsection{Real-model audit: contract, protocol and status}
\label{sec:real}
\paragraph{Contract.}
We fixed one public pair from its sources.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Model:} GPT-2 small at a pinned revision (MIT on its model card).
  \item \emph{SAE:} the residual SAE at \texttt{blocks.8.hook\_resid\_pre} from a public
  release (MIT; $d=768$, $m=24576$, float32, 128-token contexts).
\end{itemize}
\Cref{tab:contract} lists every field with its source. The SAE's config file does not
state its activation, input-bias handling or normalization.
The SAELens loader fills these in with defaults: ReLU,
$p = W_{\mathrm{enc}}^{\top}(x - b_{\mathrm{dec}}) + b_{\mathrm{enc}}$, no normalization, and a
prepended BOS token. For this release it also sets \texttt{center\_writing\_weights} when
loading the model. In TransformerLens, which performs this centering, it subtracts the
mean over the model dimension from every weight and bias that writes to the residual
stream. We derive from this that the SAE's coordinates are the Hugging Face hidden
state minus its per-token mean. We therefore define edits in those coordinates: the SAE
reads $x+\delta$, and the model runs on $h+\delta$.

Of the contract checks, only the config comparison was executed, against the verbatim
configuration files. The expected tensor shapes are inferred from the file size. The
no-op-hook identity of logits and the reconstruction check (L0 and variance explained
against the release's reported values) are \notrun. Details are in \cref{app:contract}.

\paragraph{Fidelity protocol (P3-REAL-01R).}
The protocol runs in a fixed order.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Documents are split before windows are cut: WikiText-103 validation articles for
  calibration and test articles for evaluation.
  \item On calibration data only, 64 features are drawn from a firing-density band. Their
  doses are set at activation quantiles $q_{50}, q_{90}, q_{99}$ and $2q_{99}$, and norm
  budgets at fractions of the median residual norm. All of this is frozen with a hash.
  \item Test documents are the unit; each contributes active-target and inactive-target
  positions.
  \item Methods are compared in equal-norm and target-matched modes, and infeasible rows are
  kept.
  \item We report the decomposition of \cref{def:fidelity}, the next-token KL divergence
  and $\Delta$NLL after the edit, time and memory, with document-cluster bootstrap
  intervals.
\end{enumerate}
The earlier gate conditions (G1--G3) are kept verbatim. They are read as regime-wise
curves and paired differences with intervals, and as operational rules, not as
significance tests (\cref{app:real01r}).

\paragraph{Behavioral protocol (P3-REAL-02).}

  \item The steered feature, a difference-in-means baseline, and each method's norm budget
  at a calibration KL of at most $0.1$ nats are all chosen on calibration documents.
  \item Success is then measured on held-out prompts, paired by document.
  \item The method claim would be supported only if the \LN{} correction beat the decoder,
  the encoder gradient and difference-in-means at matched quality, with intervals excluding
  zero.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{The toy results are small and largely forced.} Most outcomes follow from
  \cref{sec:analysis}, and the dimensions are tiny ($d=16$).
  \item \emph{The pre-registered active-set setting is dense.} Its relevance to public SAEs
  is an order-of-magnitude expectation that we have not verified.
  \item \emph{The sparser setting is exploratory.} It was added after we saw development
  seeds.
  \item \emph{The external proxy is synthetic.} It is a linear readout that we control.
  \item \emph{Internal metrics are self-referential.} This matters most for the correction,
  which optimizes them.
  \item \emph{No real-model or behavioral result exists yet.} The planned evaluation covers
  one model, one SAE and one layer, with a distribution shift (WikiText rather than the
  SAE's OpenWebText training data) and an unverified number of test documents. JumpReLU
  families such as Gemma Scope \citep{lieberum2024gemma} and TopK families add the boundary
  effects of \cref{prop:jump}; they are not covered by the planned evaluation.
  \item \emph{The behavioral label is narrow.} Lexicon labels miss paraphrase and can be
  gamed by repetition.
  \item \emph{The Euclidean norm is a modeling choice} \citep{park2024linear}.
  \item \emph{Some prior-work details remain unverified from the text.} For example, we
  could not determine whether SAE-TS re-encodes rollouts with the steering vector
  present.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
An SAE edit's description can be audited cheaply in the SAE's own coordinates, and the
audit separates cases that rescaling fixes, cases where interference remains, and cases
where no local correction exists. Where a correction exists, its internal success is
guaranteed by construction and is therefore not evidence of control. In our controlled
settings it helped only for sparse active sets and small edits; elsewhere it shifted drift
to newly activated features or abandoned the target. The real-model measurements that
would say how often each regime occurs, and whether any of this predicts behavior, are
specified and pending. Until they exist, our conclusion is diagnostic, not methodological.

\section*{Impact Statement}
This work concerns the reliability of interpretability-based interventions, which are
sometimes used to support safety claims such as removed capabilities or suppressed
behaviors. Auditing whether an edit changes what it claims should reduce such
over-claiming. The same null-space machinery can hold SAE readouts fixed while changing
behavior, which has been used to evade SAE-based defenses \citep{cui2026sae}. We do not
develop that use, and our behavioral protocol uses a benign topic-steering task.

\section{Proofs}
\label{app:proofs}
\paragraph{\Cref{prop:mismatch}.}
With $\sigma$ the identity, $p(x+\delta) - p(x) = E\delta$. For $\delta = cD_j$ this is
$cED_j$. Choosing $c = \alpha/(ED)_{jj}$ sets the target coordinate to $\alpha$, and the
$P$ coordinates are then $\alpha(ED)_{Pj}/(ED)_{jj}$. For $E = D^{\top}$ with unit columns
and pairwise cosine $c$, $(ED)_{jj} = 1$ and $(ED)_{ij} = c$, which gives
$c\sqrt{m-1}\,\alpha$.

\paragraph{\Cref{prop:feasible}.}
The range of $M$ is the orthogonal complement of $\ker M^{\top}$. Under full row rank,
$MM^{\top}$ is invertible and $\delta^\star = M^{\top}(MM^{\top})^{-1}t$ lies in
$\operatorname{range}M^{\top}\perp\ker M$. Any other solution is $\delta^\star + n$ with
$n\in\ker M$, so the Pythagorean identity gives minimality. In general $MM^{+}$ is the
orthogonal projector onto $\operatorname{range}M$. Hence $r = t - MM^{+}t\in\ker M^{\top}$
and $r^{\top}t = r^{\top}(r + MM^{+}t) = \|r\|^2$, which is nonzero iff $t\notin\operatorname{range}M$.
For duplicate rows $e$ with targets $(\alpha,0)$, both residual coordinates depend on the
same scalar $u = e^{\top}\delta$. The minimum of $(u-\alpha)^2 + u^2$ is at $u=\alpha/2$ with
value $\alpha^2/2$. The other rows can be satisfied simultaneously when $e$ and those rows
are linearly independent.

\paragraph{\Cref{cor:regimes}.}
If $D$ has full column rank, then $(D^{+})^{+} = D$. For
$E = \operatorname{diag}(s)D^{\top}$ with orthonormal $D$,
$E^{+} = D\operatorname{diag}(s)^{-1}$. For $E = D^{\top}$,
$(D^{\top})^{+} = DG^{-1}$, and
$\|DG^{-1}e_j\|^2 = e_j^{\top}G^{-1}GG^{-1}e_j = (G^{-1})_{jj}$. By Cauchy--Schwarz in the
$G$-inner product, $1 = (e_j^{\top}e_j)^2 \le (e_j^{\top}Ge_j)(e_j^{\top}G^{-1}e_j)$, and
$G_{jj} = 1$.

\paragraph{\Cref{prop:relu}.}
Along $x + s\delta$ for $s\in[0,1]$, each $p_i(s)$ is affine in $s$. Its sign therefore
changes on the segment iff it differs at the endpoints. If no index changes activity, then
$\sigma$ acts as a fixed diagonal 0/1 map on the segment. Protected pre-activations are held
fixed, so protected activations do not change. An inactive feature changes only if $p_i$
becomes positive. For an inactive target, the $j$-th row of
$\operatorname{diag}(\mathbb{1}[p>0])E$ is zero.

\paragraph{\Cref{prop:jump}.}
JumpReLU outputs $p\,\mathbb{1}[p>\theta]$, whose range is $\{0\}\cup(\theta,\infty)$.
Crossing from $p\le\theta$ to $p>\theta$ changes the output by more than $\theta$. Under
TopK, the active set has at most $k$ elements. If $k$ positive features are active and the
target enters, one must leave. The $k$-th order statistic of affine functions is not
monotone along a segment, so endpoint comparison is only a lower bound.

\paragraph{\Cref{prop:tr}.}
The objective is convex quadratic and the constraint set is convex. The KKT conditions are
$(M^{\top}M+\lambda I)\delta = M^{\top}t$ with $\lambda\ge0$ and
$\lambda(\|\delta\|-\tau) = 0$. Convexity rules out the ``hard case'' of indefinite
trust-region problems \citep{more1983computing}.

\section{Controlled Study Details}
\label{app:toy}
\paragraph{Pre-registration and deviations.}
The configuration fixed the following before the reported run: verification hypotheses
V1--V8, metrics, units, seeds, a 120\,s and two-thread budget, and stop conditions.
V1--V5 and V7 are identities; failing any of them would indicate a bug. V6 and V8 are
descriptive.

After running development seeds 0--2, we made changes before the reported run (v1 to
v2):
\begin{itemize}
  \item the CGLS tolerance of one feasibility check was made explicit ($10^{-6}$);
  \item a low-rank-rows check was added;
  \item the ReLU bias axis of \cref{sec:sparse} was added and marked exploratory.
\end{itemize}
The v1 sections were not modified.

A later bug in the summary code dropped Boolean flags from aggregates and reported a
median-only external-improvement flag. We fixed it and reran. The raw files were
bit-identical to the first run (SHA-256 recorded in the run manifest).

\paragraph{Regimes and methods.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Linear regimes use $d=16$ with $m=8$ (equiangular, orthonormal) or $m=48$
  (Gaussian columns). Encoders are pseudo-inverse, tied, or scaled tied, with scales
  log-uniform in $[0.5, 2]$.
  \item The active-set setting uses $d=16$, $m=64$ and
  $E = \operatorname{diag}(s)(\tfrac12D^{\top} + \tfrac12D^{+})$ with $s$ in $[0.7,1.4]$.
  Base points are sums of three decoder atoms with coefficients in $[0.5,1.5]$ plus noise
  $0.02$.
  \item Activation settings: ReLU with bias $-0.25$; JumpReLU with $\theta=0.3$; TopK with
  $k=6$.
  \item Doses are $\alpha\in\{0.25,1,4\}$, and budget multipliers are $\{0.5,1,2\}$ times
  $\|\alpha D_j\|$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Compute ledger.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Development runs on seeds 0--2 took 8.4\,s and 11.3\,s.
  \item A component timing smoke took about 11\,s.
  \item The reported run took 77.1\,s; the rerun after the summary fix took 76.0\,s.
  \item Regenerating the paper's tables from the raw files takes about 1\,s.
\end{itemize}
All of these ran single-threaded on a CPU.

\section{P3-REAL-01R Protocol}
\label{app:real01r}
\paragraph{Changes from the earlier design.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The unit is the document, not the feature.
  \item $a_{\max}$ is replaced by calibration-only quantiles.
  \item Features and doses are frozen before any test window is encoded.
  \item Equal-norm and target-matched comparisons are reported separately.
  \item Infeasible rows are kept.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Gates.}
The original gate text is preserved:
\begin{itemize}
  \item G1: ``the rescaled decoder's protected leakage is $<0.1\alpha$'';
  \item G2: ``crossing leakage $\ge$ protected leakage for the rescaled decoder'';
  \item G3: ``the correction's total leakage $\ge$ the rescaled decoder's in $\ge 50\%$ of
  features''.
\end{itemize}
The condition used to be the median over test features at $\alpha\ge a_{\max}$. It is now
read at $q_{99}$, per document, with 95\% document-cluster bootstrap intervals, and each
readout is one of stop side, continue side, or inconclusive. A large crossing share ends
the method claim but not the measurement study.

\paragraph{Stages.}
Six command-line stages record their status (ran, blocked, not run) with time and peak
memory: environment check, contract, calibration, timing smoke on calibration documents
only, run, and summary. In the approved environment only the environment check ran, and it
recorded missing dependencies.
